# Track every counterparty as a person

ADR 0007 gave a budget two kinds of counterparty account: one private Reimbursements account for everyone except the partner, whose entries named their person in free text and were filtered by name, and a Partner account showing the mirrored partner ledger. Both exist for the same reason: to track someone who owes you, or whom you owe, for part of what you paid. The only real difference is that the partner is also a Caishen user, so what you share with them is mirrored into their budget. Treating them as two features made new users learn two words, two registers, two share pickers, and two ways of being paid back for one idea, and the free-text name meant one person could become two through a typo or a capital letter. We propose that every counterparty is a **person**, and that being on Caishen is something a person can be, not a different kind of account.

- **One account per person.** A person is a counterparty account of kind `person` with one paired envelope in People (`person.add`), created by name the first time you share with them ("New person" in the share picker or the category list) or from Settings > People. Its id is opaque (`counterparty:person:<id>`, envelope `envelope:person:<id>`), and renaming it (`person.rename`) renames its envelope too. Float, overspending, rollover, and Ready to Assign work exactly as ADR 0007 describes for paired envelopes; nothing about the accounting changes.
- **Shares name the account, nothing else.** A share is `{ accountId, amount }`. The free-text counterparty name is gone from shares, repayments, history, and payee memory, so card credit, matching, and remembered shares key by account alone.
- **The partner is the linked person.** A person with `linkedUserId` is the partner: the other member of the household. The mirror, recorded purchases, import matches, settlements, and requests stay exactly as ADR 0007 and ADR 0011 describe, and stay limited to that one person; a household still has two members at most. The linked person keeps the ids the Partner account always had (`counterparty:partner`, `envelope:partner`), so the partner ledger finds it unchanged.
- **One way to be paid back, except where mirroring forbids it.** Recording a repayment works for every unlinked person from their register. The linked person is still settled only from both partners' bank imports, because a one-sided repayment can't keep the mirror equal. Bank repayment recognition matches deposits against person names, and a payee that is exactly one unlinked person's name defaults to their full share.
- **Former partners are closed people.** Disconnecting (ADR 0012) turns the linked person into an unlinked person with `closed` set, frozen as before. This proposal doesn't change that decision.
- **One register, one summary, one picker.** Every person opens the same register: date, payee, who paid, your category, what it cost, what it moved between you ("Owed", signed: positive means they owe you more), and the running balance, with a detail row under every selected entry, repayments included. The parts that exist only because of the mirror (Confirm, Waiting for your import, Record purchase, Request, Pay) show only for the linked person; Record repayment shows for everyone else. The share picker asks "Who is this for?" in a search list, the person on Caishen first, then by most recent use, with a New person row, and offers the same Half, All of it, or an amount for whoever is picked. The sidebar lists people with their balances and folds unlinked people who are even and hold no float under "Show settled".
- **Inviting a person is a stub.** "Invite to Caishen" on a person sends the existing household invitation (ADR 0011). Whoever accepts joins as a new linked person; linking the person you invited from to them, and moving that person's history into the mirror, is not built. Their entries stay private under the unlinked person.

## Considered options

Keeping Reimbursements and adding a per-name sub-ledger was rejected: it keeps two concepts and still has no entity to rename, invite, or remember. Making the partner a flag on the share rather than the account was rejected, because the mirror needs one account whose balance both budgets read as opposites. ADR 0007 rejected one account per person to keep the sidebar and the People group small; we now accept the extra rows and fold settled people away, because separate accounts are what make names reliable and the partner just another person.

## Consequences

This replaces ADR 0007's "Accounts" bullet and its considered option against per-person accounts; the rest of ADR 0007 stands. The `partner`, `reimbursements`, and `former-partner` account kinds and the `counterpartyName` fields remain readable only so stored budgets can be converted, and stored-data validation accepts them until then. The only schema change is a nullable `Account.linkedUserId` column.

When a household next loads (`withPeople`, triggered by `lacksCounterpartyAccounts`), each budget is converted once: the Partner account becomes the linked person, a former partner's account an unlinked closed person, and each name used on Reimbursements entries, trimmed, with runs of spaces collapsed and compared without case, becomes a person whose shares, repayments, share history, and remembered shares point at them. A Reimbursements share stored without a name becomes a person called "Someone"; a remembered share without a name is dropped. The Reimbursements account and its envelope are removed. Its float moves, in every month it was assigned, to the person who owed you the most at conversion (ties to whoever came first), so no month's Ready to Assign or envelope total changes; a budget with float but no named entries returns that float to Ready to Assign. Operations recorded before the conversion keep their old change sets, so undoing one of them could bring back the old shape until the next conversion.

The glossary replaces Reimbursements account and Partner account with Person and Linked person ("On Caishen" in the interface), and Repayment and Settlement keep their meanings: a repayment is how any unlinked person pays you back, a settlement how the linked person does. "Reimbursements" no longer appears in the product.
