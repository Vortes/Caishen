# Share costs through counterparty accounts and a mirrored partner ledger

Shared costs used to run through shared categories: a category both partners had, a split rule that turned each purchase into obligations, a month-end net settlement, and separate reimbursement claims that returned received cash to Ready to Assign. That made a purchase's purpose depend on who it was shared with, exposed both partners' categories to each other, and put repaid money back into Ready to Assign, where it looked like new cash. We replaced it with shares on transaction lines, recorded against counterparty accounts. Categories always mean purpose and stay private, and money owed between people never becomes assignable cash.

- **Accounts.** Every budget has one private Reimbursements account for everyone except the partner; each entry names its person or organization, and the register filters by name instead of creating an account per person. A partnered budget also has one Partner account. Positive means owed to you. Each counterparty account has a system-created paired envelope whose available amount is float minus the account's balance. Paired envelopes never clip at rollover, and only cash-backed entries count as cash overspending, so an unpaid cash shortfall stays visible until it is repaid or covered with float (#90).
- **Shares.** A line needs a private purpose category only for its personal portion; a full counterparty share has no purpose category. Choosing a person under People in the transaction's category picker records that full share, which remains the single stored fact; it does not categorize spending directly into a paired envelope. Reducing the share, including a write-off, requires a purpose category if it creates personal spending. Personal spending is the line amount minus the share. Half is exactly 50/50, line shares are editable, and there is no configurable default split. Every share change is kept in the transaction history, zero is a write-off, and linked refunds return shares in proportion, preserving the absence of a purpose category on full shares.
- **Ready to Assign.** A receivable never raises Ready to Assign. Assigning float reserves your own cash and lowers it; repayments restore the paired envelope instead. Settlements never touch it.
- **Partner ledger.** The payer's partner shares are mirrored into one household ledger that both budgets read with opposite balances. It holds only ledger facts: date, payee, amount, payer, the other partner's share, and history. Categories, lines without a share, other balances, and all Reimbursements data stay private. The non-payer's part counts immediately and is held from Ready to Assign until they pick their own category. Either partner can edit an entry without approval.
- **Shared purchases come from the payer's transactions.** An entry enters the partner ledger only from a line the payer shares, entered by hand or imported. A purchase made before its bank import arrives is entered by its payer on their own account, where it is matched to the import like any manual transaction and can be split. Recording purchases on the partner ledger ahead of the payer's import was removed: it duplicated manual entry, and a split recorded there could not become the payer's split lines without changing the other partner's entry.
- **Settling up.** A settlement always has both legs, one in each budget, and is recorded from the two partners' matching bank imports, including a payment into the other partner's card. One confirmation by either partner confirms both sides. A request stores only the amount, date, and requester, and closes when settlements from the other partner since that date reach the amount.

## Considered options

Keeping shared categories with per-purchase overrides was rejected because a category could not mean both "what this was for" and "who owes for it", and showing it to the partner leaks private budgeting. One account per person in Reimbursements was rejected in favor of named entries and filters, which keep the sidebar and People group small. Returning repayments to Ready to Assign, as ADR 0002 did, was rejected because it makes fronting a cost look like spending and its repayment look like income. Netting obligations at month end was rejected because a running balance with settlements from real bank payments is what partners actually check.

## Consequences

This replaces the shared-category and reimbursement parts of ADR 0001 and ADR 0002. Their commands are refused with a message naming the replacement. Stored shared categories, opening obligations, settlements, reimbursement claims, and expenses recorded under shared categories stay readable, and they still count in projections under ADR 0006. A purchase recorded under a shared category keeps it, and its shares follow its amount. Old balances are not migrated into the new accounts, and there is no opening-balance entry.
