CAISHEN DATA RETENTION AND DISPOSAL POLICY

Version: 1.0
Prepared: October 17, 2026
Status: Draft for owner review; not yet adopted or verified in operation
Policy owner: Zeyu Weng, project owner and developer
Contact: zeyu.weng@gmail.com
Application: Caishen at https://curate.is

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This policy defines how Caishen retains and disposes of personal and financial
data. Caishen is a private personal budgeting project intended for the project
owner and their partner. The policy covers live data, imported bank records,
shared records, service records, exports, development copies, and backups.

The owner administers deletion requests manually. This policy does not claim
that an automated retention or account deletion system is implemented.

2. RESPONSIBILITY AND ADOPTION

The owner is responsible for carrying out the retention schedule, verifying
deletions, recording exceptions, and reviewing this policy. Before adopting it,
the owner must confirm the procedure can be performed, record the retention
settings of each provider used, test deletion using synthetic data, and record
the adoption date and next review date in a private policy review record.

3. DATA MINIMIZATION AND STORAGE

Collect and retain only data needed for authorized budgeting features,
authentication, operation, and security. Do not collect bank login credentials
in Caishen. Use Plaid's connection flow for bank authorization.

Caishen uses Clerk for authentication, Neon for database storage, and Vercel
for hosting. Where enabled, AI categorization and review emails also involve
the providers identified in the application's privacy policy. Include those
providers in the data inventory and deletion review when they receive data.

Keep stored financial data encrypted and restrict access to authorized users
and administration necessary to operate the project. Do not put access tokens,
bank credentials, or raw financial records in logs or source control. Prefer
synthetic data for development.

4. RETENTION SCHEDULE

Account profiles, budgets, and imported financial history:
Retain while the user wants the account and the history remains necessary for
their budgeting. Review continued need quarterly. Delete data no longer needed
within 30 calendar days of that determination. For a verified deletion request,
account closure, or project shutdown, delete live personal and financial data
within 30 calendar days, subject only to the documented exceptions below.

Bank access tokens and connection metadata:
Retain only while authorized bank synchronization is needed. On a verified
disconnect or closure request, stop synchronization and revoke the Plaid Item
as soon as practicable, within 7 calendar days. Remove the stored token and
unneeded connection metadata after confirmed revocation. Record and resolve
revocation failures; do not discard the only credential needed to complete
revocation before confirming an alternative removal method.

Disconnecting a bank alone does not delete previously imported history. Tell
the user this and offer deletion of that history under the 30-day schedule.

Shared expense and settlement records:
Apply the same retention and deletion deadlines. Review all shared copies and
remove the requesting user's personal and bank-derived details. Retain another
participant's records only where they remain necessary and can be separated
without retaining the requesting user's identifiable financial data. If that
cannot be done, delete the affected shared records unless a specific legal
obligation requires retention. Explain the effect on the shared budget.

Partner invitations:
An invitation holds the invited email address, the sender's name, and the
sender's handwritten signature. It is kept only until it is used, cancelled,
replaced, or expires (seven days after it is sent). The daily job deletes spent
invitations outright, so the address and signature are not retained.

Operational records:
Retain routine technical logs, delivery records, expired invitations,
reconciliation evidence, and other temporary operational records for no more
than 90 calendar days unless still needed for an active budgeting feature or
a documented security investigation. Delete unneeded records during a monthly
review. The bank provider event trail (the BankProviderEvent table, which
records each Plaid request, webhook receipt, and Link outcome by request id
and error code, never by token or account number) is purged of rows older
than 90 calendar days by the daily job. Provider-controlled records follow
the confirmed provider settings; record any longer retention and disclose it
when relevant to a request.

Temporary exports and development copies:
Delete owner-controlled copies when their task is complete and no later than
30 calendar days after creation. Include database branches, downloads, debug
files, and trash folders. Copies independently saved by a user are under that
user's control; explain that limitation when relevant.

Deletion and policy review records:
Keep a minimal record of request date, verification, scope, actions, exceptions,
and completion for 12 months, then delete it. Do not include bank tokens, raw
transaction histories, or unnecessary identity documents in these records.

5. REQUEST AND DELETION PROCEDURE

Users may request access, correction, export, disconnection, or deletion by
emailing the policy owner. Verify identity through an existing authenticated
session or the established account email; do not request bank credentials.
Record receipt and clarify whether the request concerns one bank connection,
imported history, shared records, or the whole account. Measure the 30-day
completion period from receipt, and complete verification promptly. Follow
any shorter deadline required by applicable law.

For a full deletion, the owner must:

a. Stop synchronization, scheduled emails, and other processing for the user.
b. Revoke applicable Plaid Items through /item/remove or a supported Plaid
   control, and verify completion before removing local access tokens.
c. Remove or irreversibly anonymize the user's live data, including records
   embedded in budget JSON, bank imports, reconciliation evidence, operation
   history, invitations, delivery payloads, and shared records. Marking a record
   inactive or hidden is not deletion. Resolve database relationship constraints
   so dependent records do not leave identifiable financial data behind.
d. Delete or disable the authentication identity as appropriate. A Clerk account
   deletion alone is not evidence that Caishen's financial data was deleted.
e. Delete owner-controlled exports, test copies, and database branches containing
   the affected data. Use provider deletion controls or submit requests where
   needed. Provider-held data may be subject to independent retention duties;
   do not promise that deleting local data erases all provider records.
f. Verify live deletion without exporting the financial records again. Record
   the outcome and tell the user what was removed, any justified exceptions,
   and when remaining backup copies are expected to expire.

6. BACKUPS AND DISPOSAL

Before adoption, document the actual restore window, snapshot retention, and
deletion capabilities of each provider and any owner-managed backup. Review
these settings quarterly and when providers or plans change. Do not promise
an unverified backup deletion deadline.

If a backup cannot be selectively edited, restrict it to recovery use and let
it expire under its documented retention window. Do not extend that window
for deleted data without a documented legal obligation. Before restored data
is used by the application or synchronization resumes, reapply outstanding
deletions and disconnections so deleted records are not reintroduced.

Use authenticated database and provider deletion controls. Delete file copies
and empty trash or version history where supported. The hosting and storage
providers manage physical media disposal; this policy does not claim that
the project owner physically overwrites their storage devices.

7. EXCEPTIONS AND REVIEW

Retain data beyond these periods only for a specific applicable legal
obligation or a necessary, time-limited security investigation. Record the
reason, minimum data required, restricted access, and review or expiry date.
Review exceptions monthly and delete the data when the reason ends. Inform
the affected user unless prohibited by law. Do not retain all shared financial
history merely because another account remains active.

Review this policy at least annually and when the project, providers, data
uses, or applicable requirements change. Check relevant privacy requirements
before expanding beyond the private two-person use case. Update the public
privacy notice to match adopted practices. A policy document alone does not
establish legal compliance or prove operational enforcement.
